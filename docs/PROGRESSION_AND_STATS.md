# Command, progression, and effective stats

The Commander Hall lists permanent, rented, expired, and locked specialists. Existing accounts and the seeded demo keep their eight legacy selections as permanent entitlements. A newly founded account starts with the Warden King and can acquire seven other specialists with earned resources. Each permanent recruit has a specific building tier requirement. After the tutorial, any locked specialist may be tried for 24 hours, 72 hours, or seven days without meeting that building tier. An extension cannot leave more than seven days prepaid, and the next signing takes at least 30 minutes. This does not create BCH payments, tokens, or wallet custody.

Commander attributes describe real work performed in a city or battle. The Battle Commander increases opening pressure; the Warden reduces early defensive losses; the Ranger raises espionage success; the Engineer changes build duration; the Keeper changes capacity; the Merchant changes exchange rate; the Scholar changes research costs; and the Queen raises a cell's initial rebellion morale. Rarity has no separate combat multiplier. The player orders an army through a specialist rather than an independent hero combatant, so a commander does not list fictitious weapon range, hit points, or speed.

Mastery has four levels, with earned experience milestones at 20, 55, and 110. Eligible fights, expeditions, and specialist work award experience. The system allows no more than 24 experience per commander each UTC day and requires earned resources and permanent ownership for each level increase. Rentals earn progress toward permanent mastery without equipping fittings or using unlocked mastery until recruited. Fittings take an earned resource cost, a facility requirement, one eligible permanent specialist, and a one-hour attunement cooldown when switched. Each fitting can be assigned to only one hero. A finished contract does not cancel an accepted work order or reconnaissance. Territory rosters lock at the claim roster deadline. A new internal conflict holds the specialist active at its first front until it ends; later projects use the Warden fallback after expiry. A commander injury suspends their battle effects while it is active.

The state API provides the same stats and quotes the server uses. `player.stats` splits available strength, committed rosters, cap, technology, political morale, food upkeep, native and territorial output, capacity, and exchange rates. Garrisoned and otherwise deployed troops count toward training capacity and food upkeep: moving them off the city list cannot create free capacity. Building and technology projections calculate their next value and cost; training quotes use the same queue slots and upkeep as the command. Territory sites publish terrain and local fortification, supply distance, and next fortification. Enemy city army and walls are unknown without authorized current intelligence, and legacy estimates are marked stale. Public rankings no longer expose per-rival exact power. The attack preview simulates casualty ranges only when fresh intelligence contains a troop breakdown, and never uses the actual secret defender state. Matchmaking still enforces the actual bracket and protections at execution.

`node scripts/simulate-progression.mjs` runs 12,000 equal-power cases, including paired commander, defender, formation and field comparisons. In legacy conditions, baseline attacking wins were 33.69%; a level 4 Battle Commander with a Field Standard raised that to 36.48%, while a level 4 Warden with Sentinel Plate reduced it to 32.02%. Both veterans produced 34.74%; a mismatched formation produced 32.19%. Average casualty changes were below one troop in these samples. This comparison measures the embedded rule changes, not an estimate for any individual game.

At comparable composite power, concentrated cavalry won 50.08% against the four sampled build mixes while combined arms won 26.67%, ranged 38.57%, and defensive shield walls 19.25% as attackers. New battles give cavalry a small opening advantage on plains and reduced pressure in constricted terrain, with an additional 6% pressure reduction after exchange three. Across the same seeds, cavalry fell to 41.47%, ranged rose to 42.01%, combined arms 27.92%, and shield walls 20.94%; overall attack wins changed from 33.69% to 33.07%. Defensive odds still include fixed fortifications and scouting is still useful. Old combat records retain their rule version, and pending political campaigns and claims opened under the legacy version continue with that version. The [raw simulation](progression-simulation-results.json) includes head-to-head rates, mean casualties, and the recruitment comparison. A trial pricing model made seven days cost 112.8–118.0% of permanent recruitment; the chosen 4.25× daily cost reduced it to 91.4–95.6%. The permanent option is better for ongoing use when the prerequisite is met; shorter rentals unlock practical early access.

The current stats describe battle pressure, protection, tactical coefficients, and movement effects used by the engine. The system does not add individual troop targeting or separate squad XP. Rules are defined in `config/progression.mjs`, calculated in `packages/game-engine/stats.mjs` and `packages/game-engine/combat.mjs`, and tested in `packages/game-engine/progression.test.mjs`.
